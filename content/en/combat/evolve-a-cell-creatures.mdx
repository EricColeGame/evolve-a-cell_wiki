The Roblox experience [Evolve a Cell](https://www.roblox.com/games/75545101555306/Evolve-a-Cell) drops you into a pond as something small, soft, and extremely edible. Created by the group Splitline: Creative and first published on September 26, 2026, the game has already drawn roughly 2.3 million visits and often hosts around 5,000 concurrent players across servers that support up to 16 people each. If you have played Spore, the comparison is intentional: the developer credits Spore as an inspiration. But Evolve a Cell is its own game, and treating it like a Spore clone will get you eaten.

This guide sticks to what the official Roblox page actually advertises, notes what third-party guides report as community observation, and is honest about what simply is not documented anywhere reliable. Do not expect mutation prices, DNA values, boss names, or codes here, because none of those are published by the developer.

## What the Game Actually Advertises

The verified official description is short and specific. In Evolve a Cell, you:

- Control a tiny cell in a pond full of hungry things
- Eat plants or hunt other cells, including other players
- Evolve by adding jaws, spikes, eyes, tails, and more
- Shape and paint your own creature
- Grow huge and take down giant bosses

That is the entire documented feature set. Everything else, including specific mutation names, costs, progression stages, and boss identities, is not part of the official record. If a video or wiki claims to know exact DNA values or a full mutation tree, treat it as a third-party observation rather than developer documentation. The developer may change or add systems at any time, and the official page is the only place where advertised features are guaranteed.

## Your First Minutes in the Pond

Your opening priority is simple: do not die. You start as a tiny cell surrounded by things that can eat you, and the pond does not wait for you to get comfortable. Because you begin small, the safest early move is to eat plants rather than pick fights. Plants do not chase you, they do not bite back, and they let you build up size without gambling your life against a larger cell.

Hunting is always an option, and the official description explicitly includes hunting other cells and other players. But early on, hunting is a risk-reward decision. A player-controlled cell may be unpredictable, while an AI cell might be easy prey. There is no documented stat sheet telling you which is safer, so judge by what you can see: if something is bigger than you and moving toward you, assume it is a threat.

A practical habit is to stay near cover or pond edges while you eat and only commit to a hunt when you have a clear escape route. Do not assume you can outswim everything, because the game does not publish speed values.

Evolution is the heart of the game, and the official description names jaws, spikes, eyes, tails, and "more" as the kinds of things you add. That is the extent of what is documented. Customization is separate from function as far as the official description goes, so you can make a creature that looks the way you want without assuming the paint affects gameplay.

## Hunting Players and Growing Large

The most interesting tension in Evolve a Cell is that the pond is shared. Servers hold up to 16 players, and the official description confirms you can hunt other cells, including players. That means every session mixes predictable AI behavior with human unpredictability.

Growing large enough is the stated path to taking down giant bosses. The developer confirms bosses exist and that you need to grow huge to fight them, but does not name them, list their attacks, or publish recommended sizes. Community guides sometimes share boss tips, and those can be useful as third-party observations, but verify them yourself rather than trusting a claim about exact health or damage numbers.

If you want to fight a boss, the honest advice is: survive, eat, evolve, and keep growing until the game presents the opportunity. Do not expect a documented checklist.

## FAQ

**Is Evolve a Cell the same as Spore?**
No. The developer credits Spore as an inspiration, but the games are separate. Do not assume Spore's cell-stage parts or progression apply here.

**Can I play solo?**
Servers support up to 16 players, and the pond contains AI threats and food as well as other players, so solo play is possible in principle. The official page does not document a dedicated single-player mode.

**Are there promo codes?**
None are documented on the official page. Treat any code list as unverified.

**How do I get bigger?**
By eating plants and hunting other cells, then evolving. That is what the official description supports.

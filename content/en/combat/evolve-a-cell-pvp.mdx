"Evolve a Cell" is one of those Roblox experiences whose pitch fits in a single sentence: you start as something tiny, and the pond is full of things that would like to eat you. Created by the group Splitline: Creative, the experience launched on September 26, 2026, and was last updated on October 13, 2026. According to the official Roblox record, it supports up to 16 players per server and had accumulated roughly 2.3 million visits with about 5,000 players online at the time of retrieval.

If you are trying to decide whether to jump in, or you have already spawned and are unsure what the game actually promises versus what the community assumes, this guide sticks to what the developer documents and clearly flags everything else. ## What the Developer Actually Advertises

The official description is short but specific. It tells players they control a tiny cell in a pond full of hungry things. From there, the advertised loop is straightforward:

- Eat plants or hunt other cells, including other players.
- Evolve by adding jaws, spikes, eyes, tails and more.
- Shape and paint your own creature.
- Grow huge and take down giant bosses.

The developer also states that the game is inspired by Spore. That is an inspiration credit, not a claim of shared systems. Spore's cell stage has its own parts, progression and editor conventions, and none of that should be treated as documentation for Evolve a Cell. If you go looking for a Spore-style part list and try to map it one-to-one onto this game, you are importing assumptions the developer never made.

Notice what the description does *not* include. There are no mutation names beyond the general categories above, no costs, no DNA or biomass values, no stage list, no boss names, and no codes. That absence matters, because a lot of search results around this game fill those gaps with guesses.

## Reading the Pond: What the Core Loop Implies

The advertised features describe a survival sandbox with two food paths. Herbivore-style play means eating plants, which is the lower-risk route. Hunting means targeting other cells, and because servers hold up to 16 players, some of those targets are real people rather than AI.

That dual-path design is why the game is more interesting than a simple eat-and-grow sim. Eating plants is reliable but presumably slower. Hunting players is riskier but puts you in direct competition with someone who is also evolving. The description supports this tension without specifying numbers, so treat any claim about exact growth rates or damage values as unverified.

The evolution categories the developer lists — jaws, spikes, eyes, tails and more — map onto recognizable roles. Jaws suggest offense. Spikes suggest defense or retaliation. Eyes suggest perception or awareness. Tails suggest movement or maneuverability. That is reasonable inference from the words themselves, not a documented stat sheet. The phrase "and more" confirms the list is not exhaustive, so any complete part index you see online is either community-compiled or invented.

## Shaping and Painting Your Creature

Customization is one of the few features described outright: you shape and paint your own creature. This is cosmetic and structural self-expression layered on top of a survival game, which is unusual for the genre and clearly part of the Spore lineage the developer credits.

What is not documented is how deep the editor goes. How many body segments can you add? Can you resize individual parts? Are there color palettes, patterns, or freeform painting? None of that appears in the official description. If the editor's depth is the deciding factor for you, the honest answer is to load the game and open the editor yourself. Any guide claiming a specific number of customization slots is working from observation, not documentation.

## Bosses and the Long Game

"Grow huge and take down giant bosses" is the advertised end goal. This tells you the game has a ceiling to aim at and that size is a prerequisite. It does not tell you how many bosses exist, what they look like, where they spawn, or what happens after you defeat one.

This is the clearest example of a gap between what players want to know and what the developer has published. Boss names, health values, spawn conditions and rewards are all undocumented. Third-party guides may describe encounters they have seen, but those are secondary observations from players, not developer documentation, and they can change with any update. Given that the game was updated on October 13, 2026, only a day before this writing, treat any boss guide older than that with extra caution and verify in-game.

## How to Verify Anything Not Covered Here

Because the game updates frequently and the official description is brief, the most reliable research method is direct observation combined with the official page. A practical approach:

1. Open the official game page and read the current description. It is the authority on advertised features.
2. Join a server and test one system at a time — growth, part effects, editor limits — rather than trusting a single guide.
3. When you see a claim about costs, stats or bosses, check whether it appears on the official page. If not, label it community knowledge.
4. Re-check after patches. A game updated the day before you read a guide may have changed since that guide was written.

This will not guarantee you become the biggest cell in the pond, and no article can promise that. What it will do is keep you from wasting time on fabricated part lists, invented DNA values, or codes that do not exist.

## FAQ

No. The developer states it is inspired by Spore. Inspiration is not equivalence, and Spore's parts, stages and progression do not transfer over as documented facts.

**How many players are in a server?**
The official record lists up to 16 players per server.

**Are there promo codes?**
The official description documents none. Treat any code list as unverified until the developer publishes one.

**Can I play as a plant-eater instead of a hunter?**
The advertised options include eating plants, so a non-aggressive path is described. Specific plant types and growth values are not documented.

**What are the bosses called?**
Boss names are not documented in the official description. Anyone offering a name is reporting an observation, not developer information.

**How do I know if a guide is accurate?**

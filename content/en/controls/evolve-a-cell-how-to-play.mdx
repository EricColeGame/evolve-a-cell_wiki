If you have spent any time on Roblox looking for creature-survival games, you have probably run into a title that promises you start as something small and end up as something enormous. [Evolve a Cell](https://www.roblox.com/games/75545101555306/Evolve-a-Cell) is one of those games, and it has grown quickly. According to the official Roblox record retrieved on October 14, 2026, the experience was created by the group Splitline: Creative on September 26, 2026, and last updated on October 13, 2026. It supports up to 16 players per server and had roughly 2.3 million visits with about 5,000 players online at the time of that retrieval.

That is a lot of traffic for a game less than three weeks old, which tells you something about the appeal of the concept. The developer describes Evolve a Cell as inspired by Spore. That credit matters, because it explains the fantasy the game is selling without making it a copy of Maxis' game. Below is a breakdown of what the official page actually documents, what third-party guides and players report, and what you should stop assuming until you verify it yourself in the pond.

## What the official description promises

The Roblox page for Evolve a Cell lays out the core loop in plain language. You are a tiny cell in a pond full of hungry things. You can eat plants, or you can hunt other cells, including other players. You evolve by adding jaws, spikes, eyes, tails and more. You can shape and paint your own creature. Eventually you grow large enough to take down giant bosses.

Those are the advertised pillars: survival, two food paths (plants and meat, including player cells), part-based evolution, cosmetic customization, and boss fights. Everything else you might hear about the game is either an observation from someone who played it or something nobody has documented at all.

## How a typical session plays out

The official description gives you the shape of a run without naming a single mechanic beyond eating, hunting, evolving and fighting. What it makes clear is that the pond is not a safe starting zone. Other cells are described as hungry, and other players are explicitly called out as prey. That means the early game is about positioning as much as it is about eating.

A practical way to think about it: the game rewards reading the pond before committing to anything. If the server is crowded with large creatures, nibbling plants at the edges is a lower-risk path to growth. If you want to hunt, you need to accept that hunting brings you into range of things that can hunt you. The official page does not document a size advantage system, a stat table, or a damage formula, so treat any claim about exactly how much stronger a bigger cell is as unverified.

## Parts, painting and creature identity

The advertised part list is short and specific: jaws, spikes, eyes, tails and more. The "and more" is doing real work there. The page does not enumerate the full catalog, and it does not list costs, unlock requirements or progression gates for any part. If you see a guide claiming a specific part costs a specific amount of a specific currency, that is a third-party observation, not developer documentation, and it may be out of date the moment the game is patched.

What is documented is that you can shape and paint your own creature. That makes Evolve a Cell as much a creative tool as a survival game, which is consistent with its Spore inspiration. Players who enjoy the aesthetic side can reasonably expect to spend time in a customization screen. How deep that customization goes, and whether choices are purely visual or affect gameplay, is not stated on the official page. The honest answer is that you should open the game and look at the editor yourself to find out.

## What is not documented, and how to check

This is the part where a lot of game writing goes wrong, so it is worth being blunt. There is no official list of mutation names, no published costs, no biomass or DNA values, no stage-by-stage progression chart, no hotkey reference, and no patch notes on the Roblox page beyond the last-update date. There are no promo codes documented, so anyone telling you a code exists is guessing or repeating a rumor.

If you want a reliable answer to any of those questions, the verification path is the same every time. Open the game in Roblox, use the in-game menus and the creature editor, and read what the interface actually says. Then compare it against the official page, which is the only source the developer controls. Community wikis and YouTube guides can be useful for learning tricks, but they are secondary accounts written by players, and they drift out of date as the game is updated.

## Is it worth trying?

## FAQ

**Is Evolve a Cell the same as Spore?**
No. The developer credits Spore as an inspiration. The official description does not claim shared mechanics, parts or progression with Spore, and you should not assume Spore's cell stage rules apply here.

**Are there promo codes?**
None are documented on the official Roblox page. Treat any code list you find elsewhere as unverified.

**How many players are in a server?**
Up to 16 per the official record.

**Can I play solo?**
The game supports up to 16 players, but the official page does not describe a private-server option or a single-player mode. Check the game page for current server options.

**What parts can I evolve?**
The advertised list is jaws, spikes, eyes, tails and more. The full catalog is not published on the official page.
